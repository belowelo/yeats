## Abstract

Using Yeats's mysticism, Irish legends, myths, and folktales, he incorporates his own distinctive symbolism into his poetry. Allegory, usually understood as a contrasting concept of symbols, tends to be limited to didactic, satirical, or personified images and meanings, but his bird poems have allegorical elements. I understand the swan and falcon in his poetry are used and become mythical and archaeological images, and also regard the images of peacocks and cocks as prophetic and immortal qualities. Unlike much of English poetry representing the rise and fall, or aerial images, Yeats builds a poetic world through his own unique historic perspective and mysticism of the Gyres and the Great Wheel, as well as the mythical elements. This process results in various symbols: while a particular image is being used as an allegory, it speaks another thing consistently.
