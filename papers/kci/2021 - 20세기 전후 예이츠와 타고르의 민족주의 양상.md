## Abstract

Tagore and Yeats, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature, represent two different cultures, countries, and traditions, and engage in literary activities under British colonial rule. The purpose of this study is to examine how Yeats and Tagore express nationalism through their works in the British colonization around the 20th Century. Yeats and Tagore were against extreme nationalism at the time and advocated the nationalism they thought of. Tagore always stands for his own people but he has no hostility towards people who belong to any other country, religion, and other tradition. Tagore is sure about the process that nationalism without cosmopolitan humanity will cause harm to the history of mankind. Yeats is concerned about the degeneration of the tradition of his land and culture. Both Tagore and Yeats deal with the nationalism from the perspective of human history.
