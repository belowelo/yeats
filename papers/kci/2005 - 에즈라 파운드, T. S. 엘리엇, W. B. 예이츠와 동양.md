## Abstract

Ezra Pound, T. S. Eliot and W. B. Yeats have a variety of characters and their own peculiar styles. They established their own world of poetry respectively. But they have something in common. First, they all made a great contribution to make available English poetic tradition which had lost vitality and momentum. They discovered new idioms and poetic materials: the imagery of modern city life which had been considered non-poetic. They brought on the revolution of English poetry. Second, they had a great interest in the Orient and then were influenced by the Orient. Ezra Pound had a great interest in China and was influenced by Chinese letters and Chinese poetry. T. S. Eliot had a great concern with India and was influenced by Indian thoughts and sensibilities. W. B. Yeats had a great interest in Japan and was influenced by Japanese Noh(能) drama. 
But it was not until recently that the great influence the Orient had on these three poets' thoughts, sensibilities and writing was noticed and studied. As late as 1990, few studies on the relationship between these three poets and the Orient were done. The most remarkable thing is the fact that the studies of the Asian American scholars like Sri (Indian-American) and Beongcheon Yu (Korean-American) whose ancestors were Asian have been increasing after 1990. These studies have helped to promote the exchange between the East and the West, especially in the field of poetry and drama. This paper traces the influence of the Orient on the three poets and comes to the conclusion that the Orient has greatly contributed to the development of modern English and American poetry and drama.
