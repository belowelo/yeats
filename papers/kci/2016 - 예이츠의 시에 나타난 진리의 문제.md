## Abstract

W. B. Yeats tries to tell the Truth, the Word of God in his poems and writings. This paper mainly deals with his poem, "The Song of the Happy Shepherd" and proves that 'Words,' 'comforters,' and so on in the poem are deeply related to the Word of God. Yeats feels that the present world is painted with 'Grey Truth' because people in the world are not interested in the Truth any more and they worship 'dusty deeds.' Therefore, Yeats wants to tell people 'Words alone are certain good.' The Bible says that the Word is God and the Word of God is Jesus Christ. Yeats believes that if the Word of God, a Comforter abides in the hearts of people, people will have peace and joy in the Holy Spirit. The Scripture says that the Comforter is the Holy Spirit. Yeats uses 'comforters' as the Holy Spirit in "The Song of the Happy Shepherd." He also uses the 'Word' as a symbol of Jesus Christ. Though Yeats does not show in his poems directly that he believes in Christ, he indicates the image of Christ by using many symbolic words in his poetry. As for W.B. Yeats, the truth means the Word of God in the poem.
