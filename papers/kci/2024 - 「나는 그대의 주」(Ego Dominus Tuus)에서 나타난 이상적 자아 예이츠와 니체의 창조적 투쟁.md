## Abstract

This paper examines the Nietzschean perspective on art and the concept of self in William Butler Yeats's poem "Ego Dominus Tuus." Yeats transforms the conflict between the self and the anti-self into a source of creative energy, exploring the potential for artistic identity and ideal life. This paper highlights Yeats's intention to reconstruct the self through his mask theory, interpreting the division and opposition within the self as essential elements of the artistic idealization process. By analyzing the creative tension and transformation of the self in "Ego Dominus Tuus," the study elucidates the interaction between ideal life and art within Yeats's poetic vision. Ultimately, it reveals how Yeats integrates Nietzsche's concept of life as art into his poetics.
