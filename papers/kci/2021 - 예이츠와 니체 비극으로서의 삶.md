## Abstract

Yeats consistently focuses on dualistic conflict and division, but differs from his early and mid-late works. It is Nietzsche that causes the poet to create these differences. In Yeats's early poems, an individual experiences tragedy which is made of conflict between reality and ideal, and ends up denying life. After 1902 when Yeats met Nietzsche for the first time, his theory of tragedy greatly have influenced Yeats. The combination of Apollonian power to protect and save life from pain and the Dionysian power to destroy and recreate it permeates Yeats's attitude toward tragic life and art especially in tragic joy. After meeting Nietzsche, Yeats's character has a will, creates joy and affirms life even though he still confronts his tragic life.
