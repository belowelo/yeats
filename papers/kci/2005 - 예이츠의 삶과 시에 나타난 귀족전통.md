## Abstract

The purpose of this paper is to explore the aristocratic tradition in Yeats' life and poems. In his poem "Coole Park and Ballylee, 1931," Yeats called himself one of the last romantics, and choose for his themes traditional sanctity and loveliness. It is understood that he sought an ideal and heroic world and praised such virtues as magnanimity, tradition, culture, custom, ceremony, and courtesy that aristocratic or heroic figures both in Irish legends and myths and in Irish history had shown. Even though some critics said that Yeats' literary and political view points represent idealism, aristocratism, and romanticism, his literary attitude was strengthened directly by association with Lady Gregory and Maud Gonne, as well by his family history. His aristocratic tradition recited four items of virtue which had stuck to his people: "you have to be generous, truthful, brave, and courteous." Yeats' aristocratic tradition is very apparent in his poetic style and infused with dignified spirit and loveliness.
