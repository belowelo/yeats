## Abstract

W. B. Yeats adapts the Irish legends and mythology into a play in The Herne's Egg and, like Sophocles, sings about the process of transition from a god-centered society to a human-centered one and sings of human self-fulfillment. Connacht King Congal and Tara King Aedh fight over the Herne's Egg and later, at a banquet held in the palace of Tara, King Congal kills King Aedh and takes possession of the Herne's eggs and Attracta, Herne's bride. The Herne's Egg symbolizes the imperial power and royal authority of the ruling nation, not the ruled nation. Also, King Congal is not killed by the fool Tom according to the curse of Great Herne, which is like the oracle of Apollo, but meets his death by his own free will. Thus, Yeats sheds new light on the Irish heroic culture through the life and death of King Congal, and makes modern people deeply contemplate the value of human life.
