## Abstract

William Butler Yeats's poems show dynamic movements using various circular shapes and structures including the turning wheel, birds wheeling in broken rings, winding stairs, and especially the gyre. This paper examines Yeats's strategic use of diverse circular and spiral symbols, and the gyre aiming at resolving the conflicts of opposites and achieving the Unity of Being. Yeats apprehends the rise and fall of man, the world, and even the civilization as the circularity of the spiral gyre, not a linear movement of time and history. In Yeats's works, there is no ending, no closure, and ultimately no completion of 'The Full Circle,' 'The Great Circle,' or the eternally turning wheel of reincarnation that many Romantic writers, including transcendentalists such as Thoreau and Emerson, mystics, and oriental thinkers aspire to complete. The gyre is infinitely turning, encompassing dualities of opposite poles, and moving upward or downward to another level of truth. Yeats creates a dynamic poetic world of an eternally whirling spiral gyre with himself being the still point of it. That's why his narrator can laugh in tragic joy even in the face of desolation of reality.
