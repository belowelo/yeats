## Abstract

This paper attempts to understand the sexual aspects of the Automatic Script which dominated the early years of George and W. B. Yeats's married life. Noting that three quarters or more of the Automatic Script are personal and sexual, the paper discusses how the sexual and intimate contents are expressed in the Script, and how they are related to religious and philosophical ideas which provided the basic materials for A Vision. The paper also discusses the occlusion of the sexual materials from A Vision, and their influence on Yeats's poetry and life.
