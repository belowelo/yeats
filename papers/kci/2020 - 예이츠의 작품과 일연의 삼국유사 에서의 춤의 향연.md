## Abstract

There are some similarities between the dance images in Yeats's and Iryeon's work. Yeats and Iryeon recorded them in their folktales, just as they were.
This was the outcome of their efforts to revive identity through national dances. The dance images appearing in their works communicate the aspiration that people's hopes would be fulfilled. They also wish that their concerns would be removed by wearing masks and performing holy and peaceful dances. Both writers selected and recorded such dances for national and individual welfare. Using folktales, legends, and dances as materials, they made a substantial contribution to people's expressing their aspiration for living in happiness and prosperity; in their works appear holy images of Irish and Korean dances, such as 'mystic songs' and 'artistic dances.'
