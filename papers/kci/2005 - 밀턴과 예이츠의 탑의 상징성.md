## Abstract

This paper aims to study symbolism of the tower in Milton's companion poems, "L'Allegro" and "Il Penseroso", and Yeats's poems, and to consider romantic attitudes of the poets. Yeats's poems related to tower symbol as well as Milton's companion or twin poems can be called as dialectic discourses on self-consciousness. Milton's two poems have quest themes on two psychological forces, instinctual self and transcendental self. Yeats poems also have quest themes on self and anti-self. Characteristics such as confidence on imagination, visions on reality, and discourses on self-consciousness as poet let us call them as 'Romantic' poets. 'The high lonely tower' and the image of the lamp at midnight in the tower of "Il Penseroso" refers to the ideal world of spirit, the poet's loneliness and pride, as well as the emblem of Romantic sensibility and imagination. It is certain that this image of Milton's greatly influenced Yeats. The tower is the workshop to both Milton and Yeats where they search for the ideal world of spirit, isolated from the real world. They wanted to have spiritual communion with daimons or medium in the place.
