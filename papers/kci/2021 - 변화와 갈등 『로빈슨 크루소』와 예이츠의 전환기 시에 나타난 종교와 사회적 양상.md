## Abstract

Based on the rise of the novel and the changes it caused in the society and the culture in the early 18th century England and the conflicts in the early 20th century in which W. B. Yeats's Transitional Poetry is set, this article examines the conflicts between generations/classes through Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe and Yeats's Transitional Poetry. Robinson Crusoe shows the part of the process of adapting the previous social structure to a newly changed environment by connecting the process of spiritual conversion of Crusoe before and after the island. Robinson Crusoe is significant in that it not only adopted Puritanism, which motivated the religious and moral characteristics of the English people until the late 17th century, but also sought to restore piety by adapting it to a new social structure in the 18th century. In the early 20th century Yeats's attempt to unify the Irish people show rather persistent and deepening divisions due to the diversity of language, religion, and class inherent in the Irish people. In "September 1913", Yeats expresses his caution against the materialistic middle class and presented the ideal human image in "The Fisherman". He criticizes both the Catholic middle class and the British Protestant elite, and pursues aristocracy based on humanism.
