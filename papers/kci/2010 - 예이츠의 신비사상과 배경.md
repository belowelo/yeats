## Abstract

This paper is an attempt to discuss how to identify the creative theory of poetry with the value system of the universal order in A Vision, especially on some occult elements and fantastic insight. To approach rationally some subjective attempts, I will focus on two points: first, on the creative theory of William B. Yeats' poetry; second, on the universal value system of the universe principle and the occult elements of nature.
Yeats tried to seek a new religion which was against the contemporary theory of evolution which was popular at that time and the rational thinking of his parents'. It was to explore the system of universal theory that was similar to Grecian science and psychologists' philosophy of universal order.
In Chapter I, I will examine Yeats' creative attitude in some poems related to the main subject. He has become a greatest romantic poet in the history of English Literature, due to his keen insight for future on the basis of realistic life. He should be re-evaluated as a creative poet and deliberate philosopher who has achieved both literature and philosophy, and as an author who has established the unified system of nature and the order of universe by sublimating Irish legend, racial religion and occult elements to poetic image.
In Chapter II, I will discuss the value system presented in A Vision as the universal truth. In the preface of earlier version of A Vision, Yeats tried to create the system of philosophy that allowed his imagination to create freely and additionally made the whole thing which could be the part of history and spirit. This led him to write A Vision In conclusion I will emphasize that he was interested in supernatural such as fairies and ghosts because he was raised in the Irish environment; the passion toward these supernatural brought him to write Mythologies, and the great work such as A Vision. Especially the main term such as Gyre and the Spiritus Mundi became the compass for the nature and the universal system. After going through many religions and philosophy such as theosophy, Rosiccianism, Zoroastrianism, Platonism and Neoplatonism, at last he was able to come up with the occult philosophy in A Vision.
