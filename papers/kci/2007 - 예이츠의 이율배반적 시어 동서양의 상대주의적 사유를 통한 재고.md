## Abstract

William Butler Yeats's Poetic Metaphors of Antinomy
—from the Contextual Standpoints of East-West Relativistic Thoughts—
Lee, Young-Cheol
(JeonJu Univ.)

This study, along with the critical review of the three preceding studies, aims to discuss William Butler Yeats's poetic metaphors of antinomy from the contextual perspective of East and West relativistic thought which are inversive to the logos and binarism of absolutism. In "A Dialogue of Self and Soul" and "Vacillation", Yeats's poetic metaphors do not represent the logos of oneness that spawns hierarchial opposition but the dual lines of antinomic values, such as self vs soul, heart vs soul, body vs spirit, and physicality vs metaphysicality. In brief, Yeats's personal map kinetically reflects the paradoxical interdependence of dual values in terms of the gyres as perpetual cycles of antinomy. Therefore, the poet's metaphors can be understood more naturally through East and West relativistic thoughts than through the logos and its binarism. In other words, the poet's metaphors are similar to East- and West-relativistic thoughts such as Nietzscheism in the West and Taoism in the East. In Nietzscheism and Taoism, Being comes from the dual interdependence of relativistic values—a crossing and a crossing over (and out) of dual lines. It is also ontologically the community of individual conflict, interconnection, and paradoxical duality. And, it is obvious that these relativistic thoughts of Nietzsche's and Taoists' encourage us to more clearly understand Yeats's metaphors that represent the perpetual cycle of one thing from another or from one point of opposition to the other.
