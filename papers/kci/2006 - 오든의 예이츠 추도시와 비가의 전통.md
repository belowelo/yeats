## Abstract

W. H. Auden's "In Memory of W. B. Yeats"
and the Tradition of the English Elegy
Kim, Seung Yoon
(Kangnam University)

Auden's poem, "In Memory of W. B. Yeats," was published on March 8, 1939 in the New Republic, and then republished in a different form later on. Two obvious ways used by many critics to illuminate the poem are to show connections with the prose piece and to contrast the different versions. The aim of this paper is to view Auden's poetic memorial in light of the long tradition of elegies written by poets at the death of another. Two classic examples are Milton's "Lycidas" and Shelley's "Adonais." In the poem "In Memory of W. B. Yeats," Auden modernizes the pastoral elegy of Milton and Shelley (1) by adding urban images and narrative voices, (2) by modifying the expected praise and passion with ambivalence and emotional reserve, and (3) by substituting the idea of a soul's eternal life for comments about a poem's purpose and lasting effect. Some readers feel that "In Memory of W. B. Yeats" fails to do what poems are supposed to do. But elegy is one of the few poetic kinds which achieve a high purpose by appearing, above all, as a ritual act. Auden's elegy, updated with stark images, narrative voices, emotional reserve, and poetic immortality, serves to celebrate our humanity, even as it marks our mortality. Praising the dead without suppressing his skeptical intelligence, Auden refashioned commemorative poetry for an anti-heroic age.
