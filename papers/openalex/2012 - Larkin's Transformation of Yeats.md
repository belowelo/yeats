## Abstract

The purpose of this paper is to gauge the "influence" of Yeats on Larkin's poetry. The influence has long been remarked but not actively pursued. Influence takes many forms from misreading in Harold Bloom's sense to being a source of departure. Even a lighthearted comic poem contains an implicit echo of Yeats's "Lake Isle of Innisfree." More seriously major poems like "Deceptions" and "High Windows" have Yeatsian sources that have gone unacknowledged. Other poems covered include "Born Yesterday," "MCMXIV," and "Sunny Prestatyn." Yeats has been a source for Larkin. It may not be a "major" influence, but Yeats's poems have proved to have an influence on Larkin's poems. One may object that the poems are too few to conclude that an intertexual relationship exists between Yeats and Larkin. Larkin, however, is not a prolific poet in terms of publishing his poetry. The poems mined for Yeats's "influence" is significant. The "influence" should not be interpreted traditionally. When two strong poets are engaged in a poetic argument, it has another meaning signifying, to borrow Bloom's term, of "misreading". Larkin's poems, influenced by Yeats, show his anxiety.
