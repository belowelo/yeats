## Abstract

In the common understanding, Ireland has long since (to paraphrase Robert Emmet's speech from the dock) taken its place among the nations of the earth. The Free State was prominent as a small nation in the League of Nations, and the Republic of Ireland continues to play a similar role, nonaligned with the major powers, in the United Nations; it did not join the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO), but rather it has played a peacekeeping role in Africa and the Middle East for almost fifty years. It has been a model for small states in the European Union, and its identity as well as its economy and society has been changed in recent years (not always for the better) by membership in the European Union (EU). But the fact that Emmet's epitaph remains unwritten (perhaps can never be written) despite the recognition of Ireland abroad and its redefinition in the European context may be a rueful comment on its continuing ambiguous status. The cost of the political autonomy of the nation in the shape of the Republic had been the partition of the island of Ireland, and the foundation of the state of Northern Ireland.
