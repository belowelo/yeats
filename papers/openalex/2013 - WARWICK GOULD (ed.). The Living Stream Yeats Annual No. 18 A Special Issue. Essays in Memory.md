## Abstract

It is 31 years since the first issue of Yeats Annual, which was founded by Richard Finneran, and published by Yeats's publisher, Macmillan. Finneran departed after the second issue, which had appeared in 1983, the same year that his edition of Yeats's poems was comprehensively and exactingly criticized in the TLS by Warwick Gould. Since its third issue, in 1985, it has been edited by Gould himself, while Finneran went on to edit Yeats: An Annual of Critical and Textual Studies. In the current issue, the late Professor Finneran receives a mention, in Gould's own contribution, as an exponent of 'the pub quiz approach' to annotation. Yeats Annual has now parted company also with Palgrave Macmillan. The new publisher is 'Open Book Publishers'. This innovative company was founded in 2008 by three academics from Cambridge, one of whom, William St. Clair, is a colleague of Gould's at the Institute of English Studies in London. It joins a number of new academic publishing ventures in the UK, such as Cambridge Scholars Press.
