## Abstract

This chapter examines Yeats's engagement with his South Asian interlocutors like Rabindranath Tagore, Mohini Chatterjee and Shri Purohit Swami, and his own anxieties about the Anglo-Irish place in independent Ireland. It will show that Yeats's spiritualist interests and engagement with concepts like caste and _advaita_ influence not just his poems and esoteric works but also plays like _The Countess Cathleen_ and _Purgatory_. The chapter makes the case that these influences have some bearing on Yeats's evolving engagement with ideas of legacy, patronage, inheritance and eugenics through his career—in poetry and other genres of his writing— which have, in recent years, received significant critical attention.
