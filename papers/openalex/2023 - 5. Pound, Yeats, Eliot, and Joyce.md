## Abstract

This chapter highlights Margaret Anderson and Jane Heap's mixed feelings about Ezra Pound's tenure as the Little Review foreign editor between 1917 and 1919. The most loyal Little Review subscribers were strongly put off by Pound's contributions, arguing that he fostered an anti-American tilt that was decidedly obnoxious. The chapter points out that Anderson and Heap did not feel threatened that Pound might assume too large a role within the Little Review. It provides an assessment of pre-Little Review influences on Pound, his contributions, and the material of others he forwarded, which explores how much sway the occult as a topic held for him. The year Pound became foreign editor in 1917, he was already under the sway of Joséphin Péladan, a French Rosicrucian of the late nineteenth and twentieth century interested in Greek drama.
