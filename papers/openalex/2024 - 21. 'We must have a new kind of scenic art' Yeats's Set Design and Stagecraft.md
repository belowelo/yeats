## Abstract

This chapter analyses the evolution of Yeats's staged productions of theatrical art. It traces a change from an early conception of theatre as a vehicle for poetry to a more inclusive understanding of the entirety of the theatrical spectacle as a form of poetry in its own right. It is shown that the mature Yeats strove to achieve a performance that was multisensory, intersubjective and often (in key moments) nonverbal, achieving a post-symbolist synthesis through the combination of many artforms.
