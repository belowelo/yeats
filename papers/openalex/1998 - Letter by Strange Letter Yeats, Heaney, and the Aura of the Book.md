## Abstract

Rand Brandes "Letter by Strange Letter": Yeats, Heaney, and the Aura of the Book W. B. Yeats and Seamus Heaney are bards ofthe book. Their immersion in the entire range of literary production-from the actual writing of the poems, to editing their own work and works of others, and to overseeing the final form, printing, and look ofthe book- is an essential element oftheir aesthetic, philosophical , mystical, and occult projects. This immersion reveals both poets' apprehension ofwhatWalter Benjamin calls a work ofart's"aura"and their awareness of the work's relative "cult value" in an age of mechanical reproduction.1 In addition, these transliterary projects may be seen as part of ancient, ongoing , and mainly European intellectual and spiritual movements informed by what cultural historian Frances A. Yates calls the "art of memory" or "artificial memory" in The Art ofMemory (1966). In his most recent poetry and prose, Heaney has acknowledged his interest in Frances Yates's conception of the art of memory and its esoteric sources and mystical inclinations. W. B. Yeats's exposure to thinkers, texts, and concepts informed by the art of memory is extensive .
