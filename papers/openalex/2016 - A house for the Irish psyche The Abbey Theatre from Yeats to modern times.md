## Abstract

At this moment the Abbey Theatre is alive and well after 84 years of existence in Dublin. It produces something like 28 plays every year on two stages, the Peacock and the larger theatre of the Abbey. It is subsidised National Theatre and it would normally, in the course of any given year, produce perhaps something between 7 and 16 original new plays by Irish writers. That is the kind of theatre it is. It is a theatre that speaks right out of the heart of Irish experience. And that is the question that I really want to address myself to. Why did Yeats, the greatest lyric poet of the 20th century in English, or in any other language, according to T.S. Eliot, devote so much of his time and energy to the founding and the administration of a threatre? He complains about theatre business, management of men and all that. But he founded the theatre and he was deeply involved in its administration from 1904 upto 1939, when he died. The year in which he died was the year in which he wrote two of his finest poetic dramas, a play called Purgatory and another play called The Death of Cuchulain. One is a play about the dynastic theme and the destiny of the soul and the other, about his great central heroic figure who stood for Ireland throughout his life, that is, the figure of the national mythic hero, Cuchulain. There is nothing that he wrote that would not strike a chord in the Indian consciousness, national, spiritual and religious. So why did he work on the theatre? Why did he devote so much energy to it? Even when his own plays stopped being performed there, which happened more or less around 1912, why did he persist in seeing to it that others followed from him and that comparatively poor writers were given a platform there?
