## Abstract

An avant-garde playwright whose theories of stagecraft evolved through performance experience, W. B. Yeats left a complex body of dramatic materials. This book establishes dramaturgical criteria, based on the playwright's own words, by which all productions of his plays might be judged. Then, through analysis of Yeats' plays in performance, it suggests how new state productions might best engage audiences without violating either texts or theories.
