## Abstract

This article examines W. B. Yeats' relation to Victorian poetry. It discusses his anti-Victorian 'revolt', stemming from his need to distance himself from both the Victorian period and from the dangers and artistic beliefs of his own generation. It analyzes his poetry in the 1890s, which learned to be concentrated, and in so doing added to the intensity of its vision. His writing addressed two conceptions of what 'vision' in poetry might be. On the one hand, there were the properties of symbolic art; on the other, there were the possibilities opened by the increasing concentration and power of the lyrics themselves. Both conceptions dealt with 'revelation' rather than the observation and reflection that were so typical of realist nineteenth-century art.
