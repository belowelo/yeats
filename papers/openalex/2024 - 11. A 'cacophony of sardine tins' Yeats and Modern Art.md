## Abstract

This chapter attempts to address the singularity of Yeats's position in relation to visual Modernism. Tracing Yeats's evolving and often complex views on not only French Impressionism and Post-Impressionism, but also Cubism and Vorticism from the mid-1910s to the end of his life and career, it goes as far as to describe the poet's relationship to modernism as a 'violent' one,. Despite the surface antagonism and frustration often voiced in Yeats's accounts of these movements and their values, the chapter identifies a shared dialectical multi-dimensionality and self-conscious fictionality that pitches the Irish poet closer to Modernism than might be expected.
