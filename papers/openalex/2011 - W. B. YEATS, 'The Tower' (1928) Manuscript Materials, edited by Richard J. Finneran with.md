## Abstract

THE Cornell Yeats project was established in the late 1970s, with the aim of presenting all available manuscripts, revised typescripts, proof-sheets, and other materials that record the growth of Yeats's poems and plays, from the earliest draftings through to the texts published in his lifetime. In all of the volumes, manuscripts are produced in photo-facsimile (on the verso) with transcriptions on the recto, marked appropriately with signs such as [?], for a cancelled and totally indecipherable word, and [?] for a conjectural reading. In both of the volumes under review—Finneran's edition of The Tower and Curtis's edition of Sophocles' Oedipus at Colonus—the bibliographical specificity is weighty and strikingly detailed. Both volumes include, for example, a descriptive census of the relevant manuscripts and paper types, a speculative chronology of when each manuscript was produced, and a note on transcription principles and procedures. As Michael Silk has noted (TLS, 3 March 2010) it is also the tradition of this series to include some wonderfully detailed and scrupulous palaeographical detail ('WBY overwrote his pencil note in light blue ink.' 'Ink text on the recto shows through the paper, giving the appearance of further pencil writing on the verso'.).
