## Abstract

During the years 1935-9, the Australian-born poet and music critic W. J. Turner (1884-1946) became an energetic supporter of and contributor to the Broadsides and poetry broadcasts. Turner proved especially valuable, for not only was he a poet many of whose views on poetry harmonised with those of Yeats, but he was also one of London's leading music critics. By 1935 he had twenty years experience as a music critic (most notably for the New Statesman) and had written several books on music.1 Between December 1935 and July 1939 he used his position with the New Statesman to write seven articles to promote what Yeats described to Lady Dorothy Wellesley as his "great project… to unite literature and music" (L 841). In this Turner became the most effective publicist of Yeats's aims, and in his praise of the Broadsides2 and his assistance with the rehearsals and preparations of the poetry broadcasts he revealed a deep understanding of and a genuine commitment to those aims.
