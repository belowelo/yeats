## Abstract

This chapter shows how Yeats's exposure to Swedish inflections of the Arts and Crafts movement had an important influence on several of his initiatives as a Senator in the Irish Free State during the 1920s. His visit to Stockholm in 1923 to be awarded the Nobel prize made an aesthetic impression in relation to the contemporary achievements of Swedish National Romantic art. Sweden also offered a model of national cultural transformation via state-sponsored patronage to emulate. Yeats is shown looking directly to this model in his energetic promotion of public art and of state sponsorship of the arts. Under this Scandinavian influence, the poet's efforts, despite many frustrations, made a palpable contribution to the visual and material fabric of the new state. Such an influence also complicates prior accounts of the politics of Yeats's sense of cultural nationalism, and of the relationship between culture and state.
