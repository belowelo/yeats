## Abstract

This study aims to explore the profound purpose of intertextuality as it echoes as overlapping stylistic and linguistic metaphors in two influential poems by W.B. Yeats: "The Second Coming" (1919) and " Leda and the Swan" (1923).This research paper shows how Yeats moves beyond a simple description of allusion, deploying a more strategic angle to it that intertwines historical, mythological, and theological narratives.These are not simple stylistic devices but are presented as complex ideas of cyclical history violently ushering in new spiritual epochs.The specific target of the paper is to highlight intertextuality as a linguistic metaphor, for which a connection between stylistic metaphor as an essential intertextual element has been shown.The stylistic metaphors act as an active reference to mythology by unfolding Yeats' vision.The Christian Annunciation motif functions as a strong stylistic metaphor for divine intervention stripped of redemption into human history in "Leda and the Swan."The similar echoes of intertextuality at multiple levels build the terrible vision of a degenerate world ready to give birth to a blood-soaked new historical era in "The Second Coming."The paper aims to fortify the idea that the power of Yeats' masterful employment of intertextual echoes within these poems is not simply thematic reference but is fundamentally overlapping as linguistic and stylistic metaphors.Both poems demonstrate Yeats' intertextual reimagination of established biblical narrative and historical progression.The allusions become complex poetic metaphors of the philosophical tenets of Yeats' aesthetic vision.
