## Abstract

With these two enormous volumes, The Longman Annotated English Poets series has embarked on publishing the poetry of W. B. Yeats. The series was launched in 1965 with Matthew Arnold's poems complete in one volume; among successors perhaps most notably were Milton's poems, complete in two volumes and splendidly edited by Alastair Fowler and John Carey. By striking contrast, the two Yeats volumes cover a mere fraction of his oeuvre, taking us from 1882, when the poet was seventeen, to 1898, just before his third book of poems, The Wind Among the Reeds, appeared. The rationale for Longman's enterprise is prefaced by these words from its general editors, who hope "to provide university teachers and students and the general reader with complete and fully annotated editions of the major English poets."
