## Abstract

Being one of the most significant poets in English poetry and a Nobel Prize owner, W. B. Yeats contributed a large amount of powerful and various poems to the whole world. Besides poetry, his attributions in play are also remarkable not only because of the literary value shown by them but also of their influence upon Irish history for seeking independence. Unlike Joyce, Yeats's main concern is on Ireland and his literary life was devoted in shaping a new Ireland. Yet one of his plays, Countess Cathleen, had been criticized for despising and insulting of the Irish people. There was even a riot and Yeats had to ask for the police's protection in order to put he play on performance. How can an Irish nationalist like Yeats be questioned about his royalty toward his homeland and thus be accused for insulting his people? What Yeats revealed in his work, is a concept of aristocratic elegance that he believed to be the essence of literature. From the portrait of four important women in his life, the influence of Noh Drama, and the Cuchulain cycle, it is clear that aristocracy means art to him and what he revealed in Countess Cathleen is a contradiction to the blind public who was easily tempted by the ambitious politicians.
