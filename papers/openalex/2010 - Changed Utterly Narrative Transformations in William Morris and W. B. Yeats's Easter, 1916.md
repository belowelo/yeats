## Abstract

Click to increase image sizeClick to decrease image size Notes 1Margot Backus accuses Yeats in "Easter, 1916," in no uncertain terms, of instigating ahistorical readings of the lives of the Easter rebels, particularly James Connolly, and makes it her task to uncover the historical evidence and recomplicate the social milieu in which the uprising took place. My argument draws a greater distinction between the text and public reception, and suggests that Yeats's anxiety about public memory is a tension in the text itself. 2The influence of Morris on Yeats is well-supported in Autobiographies and well-established critically for Yeats's earlier poetry. Peter Faulkner has traced their early encounters at meetings of the Socialist League and the continued influence of Morris on Yeats's poetry and social ideas; R. F Foster's biography of Yeats details the nature of his early devotion to Morris as a dynamic personality and artful storyteller; and Elizabeth Bergmann Loizeaux has discussed the aesthetic influence of Morris and the other Pre-Raphaelites on his early poetry and interest in the visual arts.
