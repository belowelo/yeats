## Abstract

This chapter focuses on the fluidity of Yeats's presentation of the spectacle of the body on stage. It explores how Yeats's interest in the supernatural, and his collaboration with convention-defying performers such as Florence Farr and Michio Ito, fed into his drama's ambiguous portrayals of gender and sexuality - moving from early plays such as The Countess Cathleen and The Land of Heart's Desire to mid-period works such as The Hour Glass and At the Hawk's Well. This embodied dimension of Yeats's plays then leads to the explicit and implicit transvestism of several later plays, King Oedipus, The Resurrection and The Words Upon the Window Pane. Yeats's plays are also shown to stand in contrast to the repressive sexual politics of the fledgling independent Irish state, as is further illustrated through analysis of a series of individual productions of these plays.
