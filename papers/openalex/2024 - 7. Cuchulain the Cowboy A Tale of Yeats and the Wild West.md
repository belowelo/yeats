## Abstract

This chapter argues that a form of production or projection took place in Yeats's appropriation of the myth of Cuchulain. It points out that the latter is a rich figure, functioning in a number of different registers. Among other things, the reworking of the myth shows evidence of Yeats's surprising but strong engagement with American Westerns, including the iconic, visual qualities of the American West. Even if Yeats invariably presented his own (at-times near compulsive) engagement with this form of popular fiction as a mere diversion, the chapter identifies a surprising confluence between the genre and Yeats's version of the mythical Irish hero.
