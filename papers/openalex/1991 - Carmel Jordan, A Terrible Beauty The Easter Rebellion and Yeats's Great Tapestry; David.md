## Abstract

There is a good book to be written on the ideological significance of Celtic Ireland for the generation of 1916. A Terrible Beauty is a sad disappointment. Its history is pretty dodgy, whether it refers to "the dim past of Milesian Ireland" (p. 28) or to the mundane modern republic. Yeats, Carmel Jordan says, was "not interested in a factual linear view of Irish history, but in an epiphanic one where the Image is predominant" (p. 71); and neither is she.
