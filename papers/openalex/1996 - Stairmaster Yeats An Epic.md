I DECLARE THIS WINDING, GYRING, SPRING TREADMILL OF A STAIR IS MY ANCESTRAL STAIR I. Crazy Jane Climbs the Twin Towers After the bomb she started climbing. If it came to pass she needed the practice. Months of reading ready her to ambush the top. If she can make it up she knows coming down will be a breeze. A reconstructed modern woman her thighs caressed by great wings beating still above. "I really do live among schoolchildren" she tells him, "close smelling the sweat in their hair." She thinks of them now as one step falls on the next down flights and flights of stairs. "My work has prepared me" she says to no one in particular. Passing through narrow walls the sound of breathing above her pushes her on. [End Page 17] Once she was crazy cursing the Bishop at the crossroads about her man. What was she supposed to do? Crazy Jane wanted to know why they kept calling her crazy. After all, she was home her husband was off communing with nature her lover had left to organize the masses. She was left with the flood and dark stairs. Water poured in from above and below. She kept climbing the Tower reconstructing modern womanhood step-by-step. Lately, Crazy Jane yelled at everyone. She refused to do her job she stopped eating and began smoking even started calling her life's work stupid. Perhaps Master was right. It was labor to stitch and unstitch those moment's thoughts and be beautiful. A woman and a poet cooks fish. She checks to see it doesn't burn but at the crucial moment the phone rings and her son needs his ass wiped. Stairmaster says something about love in the place of excrement. That crazy wild woman eyes him sidelong, "Shit." Truly she is cursed. But who isn't? On the radio Jane heard that five hundred new Emily Dickinson poems were discovered among her letters. Crazy Jane nods her head. But that's a truth. Nobody knows how to read a woman's words. They say she can be proud and still like her Master Stairmaster, but nothing more. [End Page 18] It seems a Professor found 498 poems hiding in plain sight. He simply rewrote Emily's prose— memories of the words of women. Poems embedded in letters to women she loved but never met the Professor read from a Master letter— to a man she loved deeply, so he said—a very literary letter my Stairmaster might have written to that beautiful mild woman, my close friend. That Professor took liberties— the pansy is transitive—is its only pang— "How audacious," he crowed, "to rewrite the Boss, but I did it!" Crazy Jane finds herself crying her pace slows, the air closes in on her lungs inching one step at a time down that Tower. She believes one day Stairmaster will climb the mountainside ascend, wings pushing the air, like that swan and on that day when her thighs shrink to fit the jeans she bought before her first son was born her words will take on both his knowledge and his power. II. A Woman Young and Old Buys Lingerie During what became the longest week of her life, Crazy Jane was forced to admit that she needed a new bra. It wasn't the hard thumb of her nipple poking a hole through the cup of her trusty Warner as much as the milky smell that clung to its edges that sealed her fate. "I'm looking for the face I had before the world was made," she told her Stairmaster. In truth, she was contemplating a love affair and the maternal midnight of her breast was consternation enough. A bra vaguely reeking of baby vomit and sweet milk might send him running before he ever stood still. [End Page 19] So, after more than a dozen years, Jane set out for the intimate apparel section.
