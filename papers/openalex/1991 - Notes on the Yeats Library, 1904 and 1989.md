## Abstract

As ever, this addition to the bibliographical accountings in YL and in YA 4 (1986) 279-90 and 6 (1988) 234-45 is greatly indebted to Anne Yeats. Its two parts focus on her father's library at different stages. Part I is based on the photographic head-piece of YA 5. Reproduced here as plate 5, this image from The Tatler, no. 157 (29 June 1904), permits a glimpse inside Yeats's London study and at the collection of books he set out, it seems, for public viewing. Several wall ornaments and one of sixty-four books standing in three rows in the photograph have been identified in the "Editor's Note" of YA 5. After physically comparing books currently in the library with those in the picture, and checking the 1920s' card file for volumes evidently missing (such as 1.15-17, 1.20, 2.5-10, 2.14-15, 3.11, 3.18, and perhaps 3.19-20), I offer the titles of part I by relative shelf position. In general, part II lists alphabetically works now present in the library which have been mistakenly reported as missing, have since been returned, or have never been accounted for.
