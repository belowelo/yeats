## Abstract

~W eats never turned his back on writing, on its glories and its pitfalls; yet he also, ?/ in his great moments, frees himself from it, as if never forgetting that the values of existence are of more weight for the spirit than the innumerable labyrinths opening between words. We are fortunate in having Yves Bonnefoy's preface to his translations of Yeats, not only as a guide to his own choices, but as a meditation on the translating work as he conceives of it. It is, from all points of view, an optimistic conception of the art of translation. That it should be a question of Yeats is significant in this optimistic light, since the Irish poet manifests, as Bonnefoy points out, a kind of momentary but nevertheless fully rounded joy that is characteristic only of Shakespeare before or after him, in the English language.
