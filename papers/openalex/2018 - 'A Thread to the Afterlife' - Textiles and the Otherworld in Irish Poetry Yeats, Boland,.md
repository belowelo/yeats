## Abstract

This essay treats modern Irish poetry which uses clothing, fabric and textile imagery to explore the relationship between this life and the afterlife, attending to forms of materialization of the Otherworld in the questing human imagination. 1 Through their recognition that cloth constitutes and manifests a bond of continuity between the living and the dead, 2 critics have long recognized textiles' use as memorial tool facilitating healing in the bereaved, such as in the AIDS quilts of America or the Troubles quilts of Northern Ireland. 3 However, the present essay focuses on fabric motifs in poetry, less as a mode of processing and living through grief, than as a means of mediating conception of alternative lives in various contexts of significant loss.
