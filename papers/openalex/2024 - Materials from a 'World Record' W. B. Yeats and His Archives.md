## Abstract

William Butler Yeats (1865-1939) was very self-conscious of how his career was subject to memory and archival processes. The concept of a grand memory, or Spiritus Mundi, is an important part of his poetics. He also subjected his own texts to repeated revision, commenting in the introduction to the 1908 version of his poems: 'The friends that have it I do wrong / When ever I remake a song, / Should know what issue at stake: / It is myself that I remake'. This chapter shows how questions concerning memory and the archive are integral to Yeats's career. Important scholarly sources of his works are presented, with special attention being given to the various manuscripts and how they are mediated physically and through verbal and digital reproduction. Emphasis is given to the globalization of Yeats Studies and how the archive of Yeats's work transcends the merely textual.
