## Abstract

This chapter explores the role of the Dun Emer Press (1902-1909, established by Elizabeth Corbett Yeats (1868-1940) as part of the Dun Emer Industries at a critical juncture in Irish cultural history. These Industries were the brainchild of Evelyn Gleeson (1855-1944), suffragette, nationalist, and socialist. Gleeson was influenced by William Morris and the Arts and Crafts movement; she was equally engaged by Irish cultural nationalism and the potential for a new craft guild in Dublin that would employ Irish women and develop their skills. In London, Gleeson was friendly with Elizabeth and Susan (Lily) Yeats, whose creative networks provided crucial support for their new project. The chapter contextualises the Dun Emer Press exploring its contribution to Irish, British and transatlantic print culture. Dun Emer's intersections with the suffrage and nationalist movements are also examined, as well as its distinctive practice of including the names of women who worked on particular publications in its material outputs. The chapter also references the Cuala Press, established after Elizabeth and Susan Yeats broke away from the Dun Emer Industries in 1909, and gestures towards later women's publishing enterprises in Ireland such as the Gayfield Press and the Attic Press.
