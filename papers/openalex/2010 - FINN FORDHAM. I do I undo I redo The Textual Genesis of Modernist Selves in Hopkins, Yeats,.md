## Abstract

I do I undo I redo addresses a phenomenally ambitious set of questions about writing and subjectivity. Finn Fordham's central hypothesis is a fascinating and original one: that there is a formative relationship between the processes and experiences of textual composition and authorial articulations of the self in writing—or, to use a formula that echoes throughout this study, that 'Formation shapes content' (p. 31). A whole host of readings and approaches is marshalled to substantiate this core tenet. The study comprises two parts, the first entitled Critical Frameworks, the second Genetic Explorations. Part I provides a set of explicating contexts to the issues under investigation. It begins by offering a description of the origins, aims, theories, and methodologies of genetic criticism, and goes on to sketch a survey of conceptions of the self that have prevailed over the past few centuries. Fordham intends to trace the connections between the ephemeral world of writing processes and the views of subjectivity that they both register and condition. 'Writing', he claims, 'is the primary technology in the formation of an identity' (p. 16). He therefore proposes to shine a light on a medium whose importance has, he contends, been vastly underestimated.
