## Abstract

Exploring the influence of animistic thought on Yeats's work, and its complications through modern occultism and esoteric philosophy, this chapter takes a long view of Yeats's poetic output, from the 1890s through Michael Robartes and the Dancer (1921) and the verse introductions to his dramatic work. New work in ecocritical studies, along with the 'new materialism', invites us to reassess these apparently outmoded ecological philosophies. Yeats, and Yeats's modernism should be considered key in such a reassessments. A vision of the ensouled tree was fundamental to Yeats's understanding of the 'mask', of spiritual communion, of a mythic imagination grounded in the earth. His reading in comparative anthropology, pseudoscience, folklore, and his engagement in occult ritual, provided a nexus for his developing sense of animism and its possibilities for both poetry and spiritual-nationalist illumination. Through an exploration of Yeats's reading across these disciplines, and his poetic invocations of animism, this chapter interrogates the idea of 'enchantment' in Yeats's poetry in relation to the mythic resonances of trees, woods, and sacred groves.
