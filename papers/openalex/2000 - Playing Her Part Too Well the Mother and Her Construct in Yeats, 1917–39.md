## Abstract

Prior to The Wild Swans at Coole, Yeats's utilization of the mother-figure facilitated his exploration of processes of mythologization, and his elaboration of the means by which social and symbolic orders are both sustained and subverted. The development of his work saw a concomitant development in the subtlety of the mother-figure's operation within the text. The poems embellished a focus on the mother's social function with themes of the mother's mediation between conflicting symbolic orders, her creative function, and her weaving, and 'wearing', of those narrative structures by which her child's place may be determined.
