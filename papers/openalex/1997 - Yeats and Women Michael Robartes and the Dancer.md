## Abstract

Michael Robartes and the Dancer (1921), is a volume with two central preoccupations: sexuality and politics.1 It celebrates both the Easter Rising of 1916 and Yeats's marriage in October 1917 to Georgie Hyde-Lees. Yeats's initial contention is that sex and politics do not mix; that women should eschew political or intellectual conflict in order to cultivate the wisdom of the body. "Opinion", according to the male speaker in the first poem, is "not worth a rush", and in the culminating poem, "A Prayer for My Daughter", Anne Yeats is admonished to "think opinions are accursed", to shun "the opinionated mind" of her father's former love, Maud Gonne.
