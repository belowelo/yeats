## Abstract

This chapter explores how Dublin was central to many of Yeats's artistic initiatives.Against the backdrop of the broader history of the Irish capital's artistic culture and institutions, it traces Yeats's key role in the foundation and running of such organizations as the Abbey Theatre, the Abbey Ballet School, the Dublin Drama League, the Municipal Galley of Modern Art, the United Arts Club and the Irish Academy of Letters. A notable culture of sociability in the city is shown to have acted as a dynamic force across Yeats's career. This operated alongside and helped to further certain cultural nationalist aspirations. However, the chapter also highlights how the city's social circles and cultures overlapped with those of London and other locations. Thus the chapter traces how sociability and transnationalism informed how the poet shaped and was shaped by the artistic fabric of pre-and post-independence Dublin.
