## Abstract

AbstractMontale's poetic translations do not always spring from an overall affinity with the cultural or ideological outlook of the translated author, and this is particularly evident in the case of his encounter with the poetry of Yeats. Montale recognized and celebrated Yeats's poetic genius, but he was inclined to take a rather less favourable view of the esoteric beliefs underlying much of the Irish poet's work. Thus, while he wrote in 1951 that Yeats had been the 'eagle' of modern English poetry, he also berated him for having toyed for too long with 'una misteriosofia alla quale non credeva'. This is typical of Montale's declarations on Yeats, which while dismissing what are characterized as the 'crazier' elements of the latter's thought, consistently refer to his work, especially his later work, in terms which are little short of reverential. So Montale accords Yeats 'un posto altissimo' among his contemporaries. For the Italian poet the sheer melodic power of Yeats's verse more than compensated for his philosophical eccentricities: 'In William Butler Yeats \…] l'armamentario neo-gaelico e tutte Ie debolezze di un pensiero da spiritualista da salotto erano sopraffatti e travolti dall'onda melodica. Esiste una Bisanzio di Yeats come esiste una Renania di Hölderlin. Si poteva ammirare Yeats (come si potè, in parte, ammirare D'Annunzio) senza credere nulla di quanto egli ci raccontava'. The fact that here he singles out the Byzantium poems for special mention reflects Montale's clear preference for Yeats's later production. Elsewhere, he writes: 'Yeats ha scritto in vecchiaia. Ma perché? Perché da giovane non aveva trovato ancora la sua fisionomia.' The critical references to Yeats in Montale's Sulla poesia (always brief, passing references, in contrast with the numerous essays dedicated to Eliot and Pound) deal almost exclusively with this 'cristallino, miracoloso e quasi pazzesco \…] Yeats dell'ultima maniera'. Montale was fascinated by the mature Yeats's mastery of poetic form, to the extent that in 1955, referring once again to the 'poesie bizantine', he declared this 'maestro del verso', this 'genio della grande tradizione europea' to be 'del tutto intraducibile'.
