## Abstract

This class-based close reading of classic texts by Austen, Twain, and Nobel winners Yeats, Camus, and Ishiguro employs a cross-cultural global East-West approach and an intertextual methodology to demonstrate that hierarchic stratification is the predominant influence in society. The detailed and incisive analysis suggests knowledge of patterns of dominance in lover-and-beloved and boss-and-worker interactive relationships is crucial for understanding, and formulating workable resolutions to, interpersonal and collective conflicts caused by politicized socioeconomic barriers. Positioned at intersections of class, gender, and race, this critical discussion cogently illuminates texts and theories, emphasizing Darwinian dominance and adaptation, Marxian class divisiveness, and Veblenian leisureliness.
