## Abstract

This paper investigates the networks of intercultural transmission connecting Edward Gordon Craig, W. B. Yeats, and Gabriele D'Annunzio in the decades before their meeting at the Volta conference of 1934, which aimed to explore the relationship between theatre and new media technologies, including cinema and radio. While Yeats shared Craig's antagonism towards the allure of mechanical media, particularly cinema, D'Annunzio embraced these technologies, viewing cinema as a transformative force capable of reanimating the scenic imaginary and reviving theatre from its moribund decadence. Despite their differing dispositions, this study argues that the innovative staging techniques, the scenic imaginary, and the perceptual experiences produced by their works reveal a complex cross-fertilisation between the live embodied medium of theatre and the technological media of photography, cinema and radio. By analysing the evolution of images in their works in relation to the circulation of images in the larger cultural environment, and drawing on ecological and media theorists like Morton and Belting, this paper demonstrates that the Modernist innovations of Craig, Yeats and D'Annunzio are not only shaped by coteries and little magazines in authorised networks of intercultural transmission, but are also entangled in a complex ecological mesh of organic and technological energies.
