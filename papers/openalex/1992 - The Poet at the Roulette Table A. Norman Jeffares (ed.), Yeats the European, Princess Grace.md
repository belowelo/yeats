## Abstract

Yeats the European brings together the papers given in May 1987 at the third annual conference organised by the Princess Grace Irish Library in Monaco. Twenty-one papers illustrate both the challenges and the rewards of the attempt to consider Yeats in a European context. The range is wide: it includes Birgit Bramsbäck on Yeats and the Nobel Prize; Masaru Sekine on Yeats's Europeanisation of the Noh aesthetic; Andrew Parkin on Yeats and Meyerhold; and useful surveys of Italian and German responses to Yeats by Toni Cerutti and Heinz Kosok respectively. George J. Watson makes us look again at the relationship between Yeats and Florence Farr by using Ibsen as the focal point of his essay. Not all of the contributors rise to the challenge: C. K. Stead avowedly sets out to "subvert" the conference theme, and presents us with a "parochial Irish" Yeats who failed to grasp the significance of the Great War. Michael Sidnell, on the other hand, transcends the focus in raising new questions about the evolving contrast between the poet and the "bundle of accident and incoherence that sits down to breakfast".
