## Abstract

The Kenneth Spencer Research Library at the University of Kansas became a premier research facility for Irish studies with substantial acquisitions in 1955 and 1959 from the private collection of the Irish author, administrator, and patriot, P. S. O'Hegarty (1879–1955). The first of these purchases, arranged only months before O'Hegarty's death, was engineered by the collector himself, who designated its emphasis to be Yeats and the Abbey Theatre.1 The second sale, arranged with Mrs O'Hegarty with the assistance of the bookselling firm Pickering and Chatto, consisted of most of the remainder of the original library. Its estimated 25,000 items, weighing about twelve tons, encompass topics and authors somewhat removed from the central and Irish thrust of the collection.2 Because both of the original purchases, idiosyncrasies aside, account for most of the Yeats Collection and virtually all of the P. S. O'Hegarty Collection at the University of Kansas, the practical logic any research scholar must apply to cither collection will depend heavily on provenance and on one factor common to both equations — the agency of O'Hegarty himself. Indeed, we may ask, who was he? How and why did he come to amass such an exemplary collection on W. B.
