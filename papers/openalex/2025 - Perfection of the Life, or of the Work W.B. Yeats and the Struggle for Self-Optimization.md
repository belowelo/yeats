## Abstract

The quest for the best possible self was a determining factor throughout the Irish writer W.B. Yeats's life and work. Yet despite this hankering to overcome the limits of the self, there was always at the same time, even in old age, an intuitive attachment to this world which made him not want to wholly shed the bodily and the physical. The result is that for Yeats the notion of self-optimization throughout his life and work is very much governed by an acute awareness of this conflict between his various selves, as well as by an inner struggle to deal with the tension between the spiritual and the material, the transcendent and the sensual. In this article I will look at the general pattern of Yeats's struggle for self-optimization throughout his life and work.
