## Abstract

This chapter explores the influence of French culture and cultural politics upon Yeats's writings at the fin de siècle. More particularly, it considers the consequences of Yeats's Parisian visits and French connections through exploring the his contributions to the short-lived periodical, The Savoy (1896), edited by Arthur Symons. Yeats's involvement with this periodical offers a focal point for exploring broader tensions in this period between the demands of nationality and the appeal of cosmopolitanism, in the form of French Decadence. The chapter argues, however, that the synergies between the values of The Savoy and Yeats at this time, including a marked investment in recent French literature, were not at odds with the poet's developing cultural nationalism. Rather, both were complexly intertwined.
