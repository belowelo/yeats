## Abstract

Vague statements are only too common about William Butler Yeats. Such, for instance, is the assertion, made by some English writer, that 'He caught the spirit of the Irish culture to translate it for English readers and enrich English literature'. It would be interesting to submit those words to the process of linguistic analysis. Did the writer mean to suggest that Yeats knew Irish ? He uses the word 'translate'. But then he speaks of 'catching the spirit of the Irish culture'. Alas, at points like this it is the spirit that killeth. A little more letter would give life ! I know only just enough of Gaelic literature and of Yeats' poetry to embark upon a very superficial and incomplete investigation of the use Yeats made of Gaelic sources. I can make a few definite statements, and support them by quotations. Other and better scholars may carry on and complete what I have begun. T. E. Brown, who, 'God wot' is a poet much inferior to Yeats, once addressed some lines to 'The future Manx poet'. He bade him go into the 'tholthan' or thatched cottage and pick up the last remnants of the old (and now extinct) Manx language.
