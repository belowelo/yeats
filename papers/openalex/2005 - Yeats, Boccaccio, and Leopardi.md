## Abstract

IN 'An Identification in Yeats's A Vision (B)',1 Robin Barrow assures us that the phrase 'virginity renews itself like the moon'2 is 'an allusion' to Boccaccio's tale of the beautiful Alatiel and to its moral: 'Bocca basciata non perde ventura; anzi rinnuova come fa la luna' ('The mouth that has been kissed does not lose its freshness, rather it renews itself as the moon does').3 Now, it is always interesting to point out possible parallels, but the Boccaccio saying and the Yeats phrase have really nothing in common but the idea of something 'renewing itself like the moon', which would seem to be proverbial in many languages, and connected with Yeats's system of recurrent phases and gyres. Had Yeats wanted to remember this sentence he would surely have mentioned the striking image of the 'mouth that has been kissed'. 1 N&Q, ccxlix (2004), 162–3. 2 W. B. Yeats, A Vision (London: Macmillan, 1962), 24. 3 Decamerone di Messer Giovanni Boccaccio (London, 1825), 180 (Giornata Seconda, Novella VIII). My translation.
