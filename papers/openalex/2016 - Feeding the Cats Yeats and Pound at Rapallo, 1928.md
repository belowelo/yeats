## Abstract

Abstract This essay discusses the relationship between Pound's and Yeats's contributions to The Exile. It addresses the poets' motives for settling at Rapallo: Pound's vision of Italy that was inspired by his reading of Hutton's Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta, Lord of Rimini and his ideas about patronage, and Yeats's illness in winter 1927–8 that inspired a new vision for his work. In dialogue with the most recent scholarship on late modernism, it argues that The Exile can be read as a late modernist text. Pound's editorial and 'Part of Canto XX' published in the Autumn 1927 issue are answered by Yeats's 'Sailing to Byzantium' and 'Blood and the Moon' in the next issue ('Primavera 1928'). The essay argues that Yeats's and Pound's attempts to exaggerate their differences also illuminate their similarities, as well as the wider field of late modernism.
