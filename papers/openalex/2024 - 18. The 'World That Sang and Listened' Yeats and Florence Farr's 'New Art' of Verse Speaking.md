## Abstract

This chapter considers Yeats's collaboration with the actress and writer Florence Farr, which enabled him to experiment with oral explorations and aural effects through the speaking of verse accompanied by Arnold Dolmetsch's psaltery. It shows how Yeats and Farr's verse speaking practices drew on and diverged from Victorian precedent, before also being followed by modernist successors. Demonstrating the wider contexts to these experiments underlines that Yeats's ideas and performances should be understood in the context of a larger network of verse-speaking practices. The chapter also argues that these practices attractively combined both authority and freedom for Yeats, so illuminating some under-regarded aspects of the formal experimentation of his poetics.
