## Abstract

Following two major sales in 2017 of items owned by the Yeats family, this special issue (no. 21) of the Yeats Annual takes stock of W. B. Yeats's enduring influence on Irish literary, biographical and textual scholarship. As Warwick Gould puts it in his Introduction, '[t]hese two great sales and the legacy of the Yeats family's 80-year tradition of generosity to Ireland's great cultural institutions provide the kaleidoscope through which the advanced research essays find their theme in this volume' (p. lxv). Although 'kaleidoscope' underscores the dizzying array of material presented here, 'advanced research' tells us that this volume is not for casual or uninformed readers of Yeats's life and work. Indeed, it rather presumes an extensive foreknowledge of both. To navigate the reader through this volume, it is perhaps helpful to break down the contributions into discrete groups. The last section, the 'Review Essays and Reviews', offers robust, and detailed, reviews and articles of recent published scholarship on Yeats, including Lauren Arrington's Revolutionary Lives: Constance and Casimir Markievicz; Matthew Gibson and Neil Mann's Yeats, Philosophy, and the Occult; and Emily C. Bloom's The Wireless Past: Anglo-Irish Writers and the BBC, 1931–1968. More straightforward reviews are bookended, however, by Deirdre Toomey's rather subjective responses to Arrington's study alongside Sonja Tiernan's Eva Gore-Booth: An Image of Such Politics and her 'Afterword' to Stoddard Martin's review of Adrian Frazier's The Adulterous Muse: Maude Gonne, Lucien Millevoye and W. B. Yeats. In the first, we are given a biographical article by Toomey on the Gore-Booth sisters rather than a scholarly review of the two monographs, with the final paragraph summarizing Tiernan's book as 'perhaps the more remarkable' and Arrington's as 'also excellent' (p. 556). Meanwhile, Toomey's 'Afterword' to Martin's review offers a first-person response to reading Frazier's Adulterous Muse, 'a fascinating but deeply-flawed monograph' (p. 598). The review section, like the volume as a whole, moves between the scholarly and the subjective, the detailed and the qualitative.
