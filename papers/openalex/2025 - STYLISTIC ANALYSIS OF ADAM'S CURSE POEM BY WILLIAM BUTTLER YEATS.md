## Abstract

This research aims to analyze William Butler Yeats' poem ''Adam's Curse'' published in the Seven Woods (1904) from a stylistic analysis perspective. The first goal of the research is to uncover phonology in the selected poem, such as consonance, assonance, alliteration, and rhyme. The other is to reveal the graphology such as contraction, apostrophes and dashes. The objectives are achieved using foregrounding theory (1970) and the poem was analyzed based on two levels: phonology and graphology. The objectives' solutions are provided by the research's results. Significant words are highlighted at the phonological level via alliteration, consonance, assonance, and end-rhyme. Apostrophes are employed in graphology to indicate direct speaking. To further break up the lines, punctuation has been employed. Keywords: Stylistics, phonological levels, graphological levels, foregrounding, Adam's curse
