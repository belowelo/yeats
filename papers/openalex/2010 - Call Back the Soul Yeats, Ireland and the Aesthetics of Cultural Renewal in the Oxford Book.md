## Abstract

In his selection of Irish writers, included in Oxford Book of Modern Verse (1936), expresses his theories of Irish poet's unique role in modern cultural renewal. He does so by applying paradigm he created for Irish cultural nationalism to grander scale. explains his paradigm in speech before Irish Academy of Letters, on 17 August 1937. Yeats, as Foster notes, integrated inevitable Ascendency triumvirate of WBY, Gregory, and Synge into iconography of Irish revolution and 'people of Ireland'(AP 596). In poem composed for occasion, The Municipal Gallery Revisited, emphasizes that Ascendency is an essential part of new (AP 596) and, that together with rural Ireland, they can create nation founded in aesthetic values that can redeem Irish people. He writes, John Synge, I and Augusta Gregory, thought All that did, all that said or sang Must come from contact with soil, from that Contact everything Antaeus-like grew strong. We three alone in modern times had brought Everything down to that sole test again, Dream of noble and beggar-man. (VP 603) Brian Arkins explains significance of classical analogy: Yeats writes of ... type of life and art that is rooted in ordinary experiences of human existence, 'in contact with soil.' From this contact ... all their efforts were increasingly successful like those of giant Anteaus, who, whenever he was thrown down, arose stronger than before through contact with his mother, Earth (89). believed in power of rural peasant and believed in value of contact with soil, but he also believed that such contact can only redeem people of Ireland if its end is an artistic vision in an ascendancy ideal. As Donald Childs points out in Modernism and Eugenics, felt that the modern world is ... because humankind is degenerating (149). Thus, aristocratic presence is essential for revitalization. However, would write that he noticed that clairvoyance, prevision, and allied gifts, rare among educated classes, are common among peasants (OB 238). Therefore, contact with soil, with peasant, and with myth and folk is equally essential for renewal. He thought his brand of cultural nationalism could liberate Irish people not only from political bondage but from conflict and infighting. An aesthetic cultural renewal, according Yeats, could also free modern world from an abyss of degeneration and, as he wrote in journal To-Morrow, call back soul to its ancient sovereignty (TAAW 4). accepted commission from Oxford's editors to compile volume in early Fall of 1934. In letter to his wife, dated October 23rd, he writes that he has been asked to edit 'The Oxford Book of Modern Verse. A few months later, as he notes in letter to Lady Londonderry of July 1935, he finds work a heavy delightful labour. In coming months, labor would prove too heavy and he relinquished responsibility for editing proofs to his wife, although he would summon his energies to write introduction: A peculiarly Yeatsian testament of intellectual autobiography, at once affirmative and allusive, and declaration of sustaining power of tradition (Foster, AP 560). introduction frames anthology, suggesting thematic relationships between chronologically arranged selections. Indeed, structured Oxford Book of Modern Verse not according to nationality but rather chronologically, beginning with late-Victorian poets Pater, Blunt, and Hopkins and ending with Auden, Spender, and George Barker. However, does discuss nationality when he introduces Irish writers. He indicates that his work should be read in relation to their work. He uses we repeatedly. Within that discussion, he uses same paradigm he used for cultural nationalism and Irish renewal: Anglo-Irish and rural peasant working together. …
