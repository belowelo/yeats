## Abstract

This chapter focuses on the physical state and spiritual significance of the book for Yeats. It traces how the poet's preoccupation with symbolic book design developed over time, from an early fascination that sought to give books magical agency, via a scepticism that after 1900 instead more overtly embraced orality, to a measured re-engagement with the symbolic power of the book in the second decade of the century. The volume of Responsibilities, as printed by both the Cuala Press in 1914 and Macmillan in 1916, is presented as a key point of transition, establishing a position and a template of book design that would serve Yeats in his later career.
