## Abstract

This chapter provides a detailed historical account of how the real and imagined locality of Byzantium developed in Yeats's writings. Returning to the poet's first invocation of Byzantine art in the 1896 short story 'Rosa Alchemica' reveals its connection with the poet's imaginative and spiritual investment in ancient Ireland. Also passed onto the young Yeats via the influence of William Morris was a sense of Byzantium as a site of socially integrated, artistic production and high aesthetic achievement. Yeats's subsequent real-life encounters with Byzantine mosaics on trips to Italy in 1907 and 1924, as well as the neo-Byzantine architecture of Stockholm during the award of the Nobel Prize in 1923, are shown to have had a major impact on the gestation of this motif. Nevertheless, its imaginative development still displayed a persistent connection with his hopes for Irish culture.
