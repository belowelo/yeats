## Abstract

Reflecting the residual influence of William Blake, and his own conception of the poet as a sage, the mature Yeats developed a fondness for epigrammatic and gnomic expression. Of special interest here is the fact that he has more epigrams on one theme than on any other: Everything that man esteems Endures a moment or a day. All things hang like a drop of dew Upon a blade of grass. From our birthday until we die Is but the winking of an eye. What's the meaning of all song? "Let all things pass away". The innocent and the beautiful Have no enemy but time.
