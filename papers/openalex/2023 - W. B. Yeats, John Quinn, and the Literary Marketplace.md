## Abstract

Abstract The American lawyer John Quinn, who first met Yeats on a visit to Ireland in 1902 and became a supportive patron of the poet, contributed significantly to the creation of W. B. Yeats and the marketing of his work in the US. As a supporter, Quinn played many roles in Yeats's life, including those of agent, lawyer, promoter, lecture tour organizer, publicist, would-be editor, advisor, friend, and rival. Their relationship was also one of conflict, but even the conflicts between the two men can be seen as flashpoints that illuminate aspects of Quinn's highly productive role in the literary marketplace for Yeats's work.
