## Abstract

This chapter explores how the work of William Blake, an important Romantic influence on Yeats, was filtered through the culture of the late Victorian age. Elucidated are the ideas and practices associated with a seldom regarded romantic wing of the Arts and Crafts Movement, including figures such as C. R. Ashbee, Thomas Cobden-Sanderson, Phoebe Traquair and W. T. Horton - beliefs and works that might be labelled Craft Romanticism. Yeats's interpretations of Blake and William Morris are shown to relate to his understanding of this Craft Romanticism. The chapter further argues Yeats ascribed central importance to his own commentary on both Morris and Blake, believing that their individual endeavours only found realisation through his visionary acts of criticism.
