## Abstract

In 1893, Stéphane Mallarmé published a short but significant essay describing his responses to a performance at the Folies Bergères in Paris given by the celebrated American solo dancer, Loïs Fuller.1 Fuller, hailed by Frank Kermode as the incarnation of the Symbolist figure, was famous in her time for her innovative performance style.2 When Mallarmé saw her he was already an avid patron of the theatre, attending drama, opera and ballet on numerous occasions, but often expressing dissatisfaction with what he considered to be a banal spectacle failing to live up to 'the divine Idea' he sought in poetry and in the dramatic arts.3 However, on this occasion he was transported. He lavished praise on Fuller's dancing, describing her musical embodiment, entwined in swirling materials shimmering in the play of light, as that of an 'enchanteresse' engaged uniquely in mystical sorcery (208). Most significantly, he praised her solo as 'la forme théâtrale de poésie par excellence' (207).4
